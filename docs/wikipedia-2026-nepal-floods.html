On the morning of 26 August 2026, a series of flash floods and debris flows destroyed the Gyirong checkpoint on the China–Nepal border and struck dozens of settlements along a 72 km (45 mi)[6] stretch of the Trishuli River[c] in Nepal.

At least 1,259 deaths and 5,083 missing persons were confirmed in Nepal with an additional 21 dead and 541 missing in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China.[7][8][9][10] The flood carried the bodies of some victims 240 km (150 mi) away into neighbouring India.

Authorities have issued flood warnings for communities along rivers in the Nepali districts of Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, and Chitwan.[11]

The precipitating geological event, thought to have been a glacier collapse on Langtang Lirung in the Himalayas, caused a chain reaction whose tremors were detected by seismometers worldwide, with magnitudes reaching Ms 5.2.[12][13]

Background

Geography and history

The Trishuli River[c] is joined by the Lhende Khola (also known as Lende River or Donglin Tsangpo in Lhasa Tibetan), which originates in Gyirong County, Tibet Autonomous Region before entering Nepal. It then flows past Dhunche, downstream from which it forms part of the Trishuli River system.[14][15]

The flash floods and mudslides occurred around the China–Nepal border crossing at Gyirong Port, a strategic border crossing connecting Nepal's Rasuwa District and Gyirong County, located at the confluence of the Trishuli and Lhende Khola rivers. The crossing saw large amounts of daily traffic related to both commerce and tourism, especially the pilgrimage tour to Mount Kailash, a holy site in Bon, Buddhism, Hinduism and Jainism.[16] The Gyirong crossing accounted for 30% of all Nepal-China trade before the disaster, and more than 100,000 people had crossed the crossing in the first half of 2026 according to Chinese media.[17]

Since 1900, dozens of floods have occurred near the border between Nepal and China.[18] Langtang Lirung, near the site of the 2026 landslide, experienced a major glacier collapse and debris avalanche triggered by the April and May 2015 Nepal earthquakes. The landslide in the Langtang valley destroyed and buried several villages, killing over 350 people.[19] A July 2025 flood caused by a glacial lake outburst washed away the bridge at the border crossing.[20][21]

Analysis of satelite imagery found that from 8 January 2026 to 18 August 2026, the glacier on Langtang Lirung moved around 10 millimetres per month but the rate accelerated weeks prior to the glacier collapse.[22][23]

Climate change

Further information: Effects of climate change on the water cycle
See also: Retreat of glaciers since 1850

Climate change contributes to the melting of permafrost, which in turn increases the likelihood of glacier collapses. Alton C. Byers, a senior research associate at the Institute of Arctic and Alpine Research, University of Colorado Boulder, said, "Permafrost has been the cryospheric glue that's held the rock and the ice and the glaciers together for millennia, and now we're getting increasing evidence that it's being weakened by warming trends."[18]

Some scientists have not directly attributed the disaster to climate change, noting more proximate causes.[18] However, other scientists have noted that climate change probably increased the likelihood of this event occurring.[24][25][26][27] Nepal foreign minister Shisir Khanal called for increased global response to climate change and stated that Nepal was bearing a disproportionate share of its consequences.[28]

Early warning systems

Chinese and Nepali scientists have maintained a flash flood early warning system for years, which the South China Morning Post credited with consistently preventing casualties.[29] However, the system proved incapable of detecting the August 2026 disaster in time as the water level monitors were optimised for detecting monsoon floods or perennial floods.[30] Upstream monitoring stations in Nepal were swept away by the flooding before alerts could be sent.[31] A senior fellow at the Stimson Center remarked that the affected region was a monitoring "blind spot" and that geologists paid little attention to it.[30]

After the 2025 floods, Chinese authorities in Gyirong County took a number of measures in preparation for a new glacial lake outburst flood. The local government updated emergency plans, set up a sentry system monitoring water level changes, constructed flood prevention structures such as a new flood wall, dikes and channels, trained local officials in "glacial lake outburst defense", and simulated an emergency evacuation of villagers. However, the August 2026 flood, which was caused by a different event than the system was built to anticipate, overwhelmed these defenses within minutes.[32]

The incident began at around 08:37 NPT. At 09:00, the Nepalese Department of Hydrology and Meteorology was informed of the flood by the District Administrative Officer in Rasuwa, by which time several communications towers had been destroyed and door-to-door warnings were being sent.[33] The first text alerts were sent out by 09:15, with 679,295 warning text messages sent within one minute.[34]

Cause

Further information: Timeline of the 2026 Nepal floods

No rain was reported that morning at the Rasuwa district headquarters. Nepal's foreign minister Shisir Khanal initially said that an earthquake triggered an avalanche which caused the flooding.[35] However, later analysis by the United States Geological Survey (USGS) indicated that the seismic signal did not precede the collapse but was rather generated by the collapse itself, meaning it was not a tectonic earthquake.[36] Other early analyses suspected a glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF).[37][38]

A seismic event measuring Ms 5.2 occurred at 02:52 UTC (08:37 NPT; 10:52 CST) and was centred just north of Langtang Lirung, a mountain in Langtang National Park.[39] The USGS' analysis of long-period seismic waves demonstrated, however, that they were consistent with a glacier collapse, not an earthquake.[40] The GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences (GFZ), in Potsdam, Germany, recorded an event measuring Mw 5.7 that was consistent with a landslide with a timestamp identical to the USGS.[1]

A second landslide event was identified by USGS three hours after the first by analyzing low-frequency seismic waves. The second landslide was recorded as a Ms 4.2 seismic event.[41]

Seven minutes after (vis. 2026 Nepal floods timeline) the seismic event, a surveillance camera[42][43][44] captured floodwaters destroying the Gyirong Port border crossing.[45][46] Such was the scale and speed of the flood, the leading hypothesis of geomorphologists is that the collapsed portion of a glacier fell thousands of metres[27] and melted from the velocity and friction of the impact. The meltwater and rocks then displaced the river in the valley.[47][48]

There were indications of heavy water flow almost immediately after the seismic waves were detected. The combination of the two near-simultaneous signals, and review of the first satellite imagery, led researchers to conclude that a glacier located around 64 kilometres (40 mi) north of the capital city Kathmandu and some 15 km (9.3 mi) east of where the flood was first noticed, measuring 600 metres (2,000 ft) in width may have fallen 1,200 metres (4,000 ft),[24][49] in what is known as a glacier collapse (also called an ice avalanche).[50] According to Daniel Shugar, a geologist at the University of Calgary in Canada who reviewed satellite imagery after the event, a 0.2 km2 (49-acre) piece of ice and rock broke off at about 5.2 km (3.2 mi) above sea level and fell vertically about 1.2 km (0.75 mi) to the valley below.[51][52]

Satellite images and seismology confirm that a glacier collapsed,[53][54] which itself is a likely result of destabilisation by tectonic uplift and climate warming.[55] Compared to GLOFs, glacier collapse floods are less frequent in the region, but more destructive.[56] The collapsed glacial material was believed to have originated from an altitude of 5,200–5,400 m (17,100–17,700 ft)[57] and had a size of around 100,000,000–200,000,000 m3 (3.5×109–7.1×109 cu ft).[58] It then moved north-westwards before shifting west.[24]

The debris flowed up to some 100 km (62 mi) downstream.[59]

Risk of further flooding

Two barrier lakes formed following the glacier collapse.[60] On 27 August, Chinese authorities warned that one of the lakes with a volume of 2,000,000 m3 (71,000,000 ft3) had formed near the confluence of the Chhochen Khola and Purepu Tsangpo rivers, adding that the possibility of a breach was high due to an expected water inflow of 3,000,000 m3 (110,000,000 ft3) until 30 August.[61] The lake overflowed slightly on 28 August, prompting Chinese authorities to briefly pause rescue operations and warn about the potential for additional flooding.[62][63] The barrier lake slowly drained out and was largely empty by 30 August.[28] The other lake was situated at a high elevation, near where the glacier collapsed.[64] Flood warning notices were sent on 30 August after Chinese authorities reported increased water flow upstream.[28]

Casualties

According to Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) on 3 September, at least 1,259 people died, more than 5,000 others were injured and 5,083 were missing in Nepal.[7][4] As of 2 September, 590 foreign nationals from 39 countries are among the missing.[65] According to the Ministry of Health on 1 September, just 4% of the roughly 900 bodies found so far had been identified.[66] The NDRRMA said on 2 September that nearly 1.6 million people had been affected by the floods.[67]

Chinese state media reported on 21 deaths and 541 missing on 3 September,[7] earlier reporting that there were 261 foreign nationals from 23 countries in Tibet.[28][8] This includes 104 Nepali nationals.[68]

Among those missing as of 2 September are over 900 workers on hydropower projects in Nepal.[69] Of the total number missing, it is believed that there are 288 Indian nationals, 127 Nepali tourists, 75 Americans,[9] 53 Ukrainians, 51 Malaysians, and 38 Australians.[65] On 26 August, it was reported that there were 33 UK nationals missing.[70][71] The Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs reported nearly 100 Chinese citizens in Nepal missing.[36][72] Australia's foreign minister said on 27 August that at least 34 of the missing were pilgrims.[36][73] A group of 77 Isha Foundation pilgrims from 19 different nationalities traveling to Tibet were at Gyirong Port during the flash flood.[74] The Canadian Government reported on 29 August that 32 citizens are missing.[75] The South Korean Foreign Ministry reported 9 citizens working at a hydropower plant construction site went missing.[76] The Latvian Foreign Ministry reported 33 Latvians missing on 29 August.[77][78]

At least 147 government officials, including eight local elected representatives, 32 local government employees, 11 teachers,[79] 28 police officers, 13 Armed Police Force personnel, 44 Nepali Army personnel, 15 customs office employees, and 4 immigration office employees were reported missing.[80] An estimated 300 trade and transportation workers were reported missing.[81] 900 people involved in 12 hydropower projects were reported missing, with 500 believed to be trapped in tunnels.[82] At the Upper Trishuli 1 Hydroelectric Project, at least 576 workers were reported missing, with around 300 trapped inside the project's tunnel.[83]

The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies estimated that 93,000 people were impacted by the floods[84] and the United Nations estimated that 65,000 people were in need of emergency water, sanitation and hygiene materials.[83] UN spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric stated that an estimated 2,600 children younger than 5 required treatment for severe wasting and 10,500 pregnant and breastfeeding women required nutrition assistance.[83] The flood carried the bodies of some victims 240 km (150 mi) away,[85] with bodies recovered as far downstream as Nawalparasi District in Nepal[86] and Kushinagar and Maharajganj districts in Uttar Pradesh, India.[87]

Damage

General

It was estimated that the resulting debris traveled at a speed of around 50 m/s (180 km/h; 110 mph),[88] while analysis of satellite images suggests that discolorations caused by the flood reached an elevation of 800 m (2,600 ft) in a valley some 7 km (4.3 mi) from the origin.[89] Following the flood, more than 1.5 m (4 ft 11 in) of silt was deposited in the main disaster zone.[90] The United Nations Development Programme estimated that the flooding generated 2.2 million tonnes of debris, including 556,200 tonnes of building debris.[91]

Floodwaters reached settlements as high as 80 metres (270 ft) above the river level.[92]

Nepal

In Nepal, residential areas and infrastructure, including roads, sustained extensive damage.[93][94] The districts of Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, and Chitwan were impacted by the flooding.[95] Around 8,317 houses were destroyed.[96] In Nuwakot, floodwater entered areas including Timure, Dhunche, Mailung, Syafrubesi, Betrawati, Trishuli, and Devighat.[92][94][97] A preliminary estimate by the Nepal government reported that the floods caused Rs 200 billion in damages.[5]

Director of the Roads Department Bijaya Jaishi reported an initial estimate of Rs 15 billion in road and bridge infrastructure had been damaged,[98] affecting 41 bridges and 42 km (26 mi) of road.[98][5] A road connecting Syafrubesi and Rasuwagadhi, part of the Trishuli Corridor (NH42), which was under reconstruction after damage from flooding in 2025, was further damaged.[98] A bridge connected to Trishuli Bazar was swept away.[99] A section of the Prithvi Highway from Baireni to Mugling was blocked until 28 August,[100] while another section of the highway between Mugling and Kathmandu was closed due to erosion from the Trishuli River.[101]

Four flood monitoring stations were destroyed.[34][102] Around 1,200 people from at least three villages were displaced.[36] The rural municipality offices in Gosaikunda Rural Municipality and Uttargaya Rural Municipality were washed away.[103] Finance minister Swarnim Wagle estimated that preliminary rebuilding costs could reach रू 762,000,000,000 (US$5,000,000,000).[104]

The education ministry said at least 20 schools were damaged in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts.[105] The Rasuwagadhi customs office was destroyed,[92] while more than 300 vehicles queuing up at the site were swept away,[106] along with around 1,000 electric vehicles and containers with spare parts being exported into Nepal.[107] An estimated 100 to 200 loaded shipping containers were in the customs yard at the time of the flood and are assumed missing.[81] Nepal Telecom said 110 of its towers had been damaged, while Ncell said it sustained damage at 78 sites[4] and losses of more than Rs90 million.[108]

Power infrastructure

Damage to electricity generation, transmission, and distribution infrastructure disrupted supplies in affected areas. The Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) said that 13 hydropower projects had been damaged, leaving hundreds of billions of rupees in damage.[109] These included Rasuwagadhi, Chilime, Trishuli 3A, the Trishuli 3B Hub 220 kV substation, Trishuli Hydropower Station, and Devighat Hydropower Station.[110] The NEA reported that damage to energy infrastructure had removed an estimated 431 megawatts of electricity off of the grid and that projects under construction with a total capacity of 470 megawatts were damaged.[5]

Over 900 workers on the hydropower projects were believed to missing as of 2 September. Australian tunnel rescue expert Arnold Dix, who was asked by Nepalese authorities to assist with rescue efforts at Upper Trishuli 3A Hydropower Station, where 42 workers are reported missing, said that 27 power stations were "wiped out", with 12 "destroyed, [with] nothing left at the surface".[69]

China

In China, road access, communications, and electricity supply to the Gyirong Port area were cut off by the flood,[111][112] affecting a section of China National Highway 216.[113] Chinese rescue workers who reached the area on 28 August confirmed that the border crossing complex was destroyed, with one describing the scene as "nothing but ruins".[114] Twenty-seven buildings were destroyed in the area.[115] Around 80 trucks and 170 containers were stranded in the town of Gyirong after flooding destroyed about 5 km (3.1 mi) of road running from the town to Rasuwagadhi.[116][117]

Further disruptions occurred on 31 August, when Chinese authorities closed the Tatopani border crossing due to risks of landslides, leaving the Korala border crossing in Mustang District as the only open thoroughfare across the Nepal-China border.[118]

Response

Nepal

The Government of Nepal mobilised the Nepali Army, Armed Police Force, and Nepal Police, supported by helicopters and medical teams, to conduct search, rescue, and relief operations.[119][120] More than 21,011 security personnel were deployed to assist rescue efforts, 8,896 personnel from the army, 7,912 from the police, and 4,203 from the APF.[67][121] Bidur hosted the primary staging ground for the army's search and rescue operations.[122] The Council of Ministers declared the area a disaster-hit zone for three months under the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act, 2074 (2017).[123][124] The government also offered free medical treatment for those injured and financial assistance to families who lost members.[125][123] On 27 August, Prime Minister Balen Shah arrived in Nuwakot District to monitor the situation.[126] The Prime Minister's Natural Disaster Relief Fund was launched by the government, raising over रू 7,770,000,000 by 3 September. A separate account for dollar donations raised at least $6 million.[127] Shah and his cabinet also pledged to donate one month's salary to the fund.[128] The government also released रू 675,000,000 to 15 local units in five affected districts from its Disaster Management Fund.[129] It also requested assistance from the Loss and Damage Fund.[130]

Nepali officials reported that over 11,814 people had been rescued, including 324 foreign nationals.[82][65] As hospitals and morgues in affected areas reached full capacity, injured survivors and cadavers were transferred to other locations including Bhairahawa, Bharatpur, Butwal, Gorkha, Kathmandu, Nawalparasi, Palpa, and Pokhara.[86] Three people died while receiving medical care.[67] As of 29 August, at least 675 bodies were recovered.[131] Due to a lack of cold storage, many unidentified bodies were temporarily buried until they could be identified,[132] with DNA testing and facial photographs used to identify bodies.[133][134] By 30 August, an estimated 100 bodies were temporarily buried in "hastily dug trenches" in Chitwan.[134] Some family members of the victims practice criticised the practice, alleging that it ignored traditional Hindu funeral traditions.[134] Other families who had not found their deceased relatives resorted to using effigies made with kusha grass in their funeral rituals.[135]

Displaced persons camps were set up in Nuwakot and Dhading.[136] In Trishuli Bazar, residents reported looting in houses with very few police officers to maintain order.[137] Several days after the disaster, headteacher Rajendra Dawadi was praised for evacuating 900 children, from Tribhuvan Trishuli secondary school in Nuwakot District, ten minutes before the flash flood. He had received four warnings from different sources, including one from the town of Betrawati, directly upstream of the school.[138][139]

Following the flooding, the Nepal Roads Department began installing bailey bridges to replace collapsed bridges.[98] The Nepal Army delivered eight tonnes of relief supplies to Rasuwa by 27 August.[140] The government launched a psychosocial counseling and telemedicine services program, with over 50 psychiatrists, psychologists, and psychosocial counselors deployed in Rasuwa and Nuwakot.[141][142] It also sent 500 portable solar systems to Rasuwa as part of efforts to provide electricity to households,[143] and deployed a fuel tanker to Trishuli for supplying aircraft engaged in relief efforts.[144]

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs initially turned down offers from several countries to send their search-and-rescue teams to support relief operations, citing mismanagement and poor coordination from the joint rescue effort following the 2015 earthquake as the main reason for the decision.[145][146] However, the Nepalese government allowed for the entry of experts from India, China, Australia, and South Korea for help in tunnel rescues, DNA testing and forensic examinations.[147] It also requested heavy-lift cargo drones from international partners to deliver relief supplies to isolated mountain regions.[148]

On 29 August, the NDRRMA said that over 18,700 rescuers had been deployed to help with the search operation.[87] Rescue efforts were hampered by heavy rains[149] and the absence of aviation assets by the Nepal Police.[150]

In the early hours of 31 August, a controlled explosion was carried out in a tunnel at the Upper Trishuli 3A Hydropower Station, in an effort to locate 933 missing workers. The Nepali Army then dug a hole about 10 m (33 ft) deep, using two excavators, and began to clear water, mud and debris from the tunnel.[151]

China

CCP general secretary Xi Jinping called for continued efforts to locate missing people and improvements to disaster monitoring and warning systems.[152][153] The government of the Tibet Autonomous Region mobilised nearly 800 emergency workers and 150 vehicles as well as sniffer dogs and speed boats to help the rescue efforts.[154] Units of the People's Armed Police,[155] the People's Liberation Army and rescue teams from neighbouring Sichuan province were deployed to assist in rescue operations.[156][84] The National Fire and Rescue Administration also sent 681 personal and 13 rescue dogs, while the Ministry of Emergency Management sent 60 personnel.[157] Two people were rescued at Gyirong Port. Access to the site was hampered by the accumulation of debris about 3 km (1.9 mi) upstream.[158] Chinese state media reported that 499 residents and 555 tourists had been evacuated from the area in Tibet.[84]

Premier Li Qiang arrived in Gyirong on 26 August.[159][36] The China Meteorological Administration set up portable automatic weather stations in Gyirong Port.[160] A 4G/5G base station was installed in Gyirong County to remediate disrupted communication infrastructure.[161] The China National Space Administration stated that it coordinated and mobilised satellite resources to perform emergency imaging of the impacted area.[162] Rescue operator China Anneng deployed 158 personnel and 33 heavy equipment to repair roads.[113] Road repair efforts were hampered by river currents flowing up ​to six times faster than normal river speeds.[113] A makeshift road connecting to Gyirong Port was completed by 2 September.[65] Chinese authorities arranged for the repatriation of least 114 Nepalis who were stranded in China through the Tatopani border crossing.[163]

On the morning of 30 August, the Government of China delivered 50 tonnes of emergency relief supplies to Kathmandu via two Xi'an Y-20 transport aircraft. The supplies were handed over by Chinese ambassador Zhang Maoming to Nepalese science minister Mahabir Pun.[164] Later that day, Ambassador Zhang Maoming handed over US$2,000,000 in emergency flood-relief assistance from the Government of China to Nepalese Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle. He also handed over an additional US$200,000 contributed by the Red Cross Society of China to Nepal's Prime Minister Disaster Relief Fund.[165] On 1 September, a second batch of relief supplies was delivered to Nepal from China, including drones, DNA testing equipment, and water purification equipment.[166] The government also deployed a team of tunnel experts to aid rescue efforts at hydropower station tunnels and a team of forensics experts to Nepal.[165][167]

Censorship and state media coverage

Further information: Censorship in China

Videos of the disaster taken by locals in Tibet and eyewitness accounts were censored on Chinese social media platforms.[168][169][170] Foreign journalists are not permitted to enter the region except on government-supervised trips, and Tibetans can face punishment for speaking to them.[168][171] Smaller Chinese state-media outlets initially published interviews with witnesses, but the articles were removed within hours.[172] The Ministry of Public Security said at least 10 people were penalised for sharing misleading or disruptive posts about the disaster.[173][174][175][176] China tightly controls information in Tibet, which it annexed in 1951, where protests over independence are suppressed. Chinese state media carried several broadcasts about the disaster and sent journalists to the affected area, although reports focused mainly on rescue efforts rather than the extent of the devastation.[171] The journalists as well as social media influencers were warned against reporting casualties different from officially announced figures.[169] A BBC headline claiming that coverage of the disaster was fully censored in China was changed to "heavily censored" after backslash from Chinese netizens.[177][170] Several foreign journalists said that Chinese authorities denied their requests to visit the affected areas citing difficult terrain and the risk of more floods.[172]

Restrictions on independent reporting made the extent of the damage on the Tibetan side difficult to verify.[171][178] The International Campaign for Tibet claimed that the casualties in Tibet were higher than what China reported, with up to 300 homes destroyed.[173] The deputy Asia director of Human Rights Watch said that Chinese state media journalists were told not to interview survivors or members of their family.[169] In response to accusations, Guo Jiakun, spokesperson of China's foreign ministry, objected to using the disaster for "smear campaigns and hype".[173]

Misinformation

The flash flood was initially attributed in some reports and social media posts to Chinese infrastructure projects, dam construction, or hydrological activity in Tibet.[d][improper synthesis?] Subsequent satellite imagery and scientific analyses, however, identified a glacier collapse on the Nepalese side of the border near Langtang Lirung as the immediate source of the flood. Hydrologists noted that detecting and providing advance warning for such high-altitude glacier-related failures is difficult. The flood reportedly reached Gyirong Port within minutes of the collapse, leaving little time for conventional flood-warning and evacuation measures.[224]

Press Council Nepal launched a portal to report flood misinformation and ordered at least 72 users to remove content, including AI-generated footage and graphic images.[225][226]
